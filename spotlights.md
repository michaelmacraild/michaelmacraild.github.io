  <div><p class="section-label">In-silico trials</p><h2>Toward safe, effective and equitable flow-diverter treatment</h2><p>This article explores how in-silico trials can support safer, more effective and more equitable flow-diverter treatment of intracranial aneurysms. Virtual patient populations bring together variation in anatomy and physiology with simulations of treatment and blood flow, allowing device performance to be explored across different clinical scenarios. The work considers how this approach can strengthen the evidence available for evaluating treatment across diverse patient populations.</p><p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wneu.2024.123589">Read the paper</a></p></div>

The work uses patient-specific simulation to investigate treatment efficacy and produce evidence for less-studied clinical scenarios and demographics.</p><p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1136/jnis-2024-022000">Read the paper</a></p></div>

  <div><p class="section-label">Simulation acceleration</p><h2>Machine learning reduced-order models for aneurysm flow</h2><p>This work developed a non-intrusive parametric model for time-dependent blood flow in intracranial aneurysms. Proper orthogonal decomposition reduces the high-fidelity simulation data, while neural networks predict the reduced coefficients across varying conditions. The best configuration achieved mean test accuracies of 98.6% in the parent vessel and 97.6% in the aneurysm.</p><p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/cnm.3848">Read the paper</a></p></div>
</section>

  <div><p class="section-label">Simulation acceleration</p><h2>Accelerating computational vascular-flow modelling</h2><p>This study reviews reduced-order and machine-learning methods for speeding up computational vascular flow modelling. It compares zero- and one-dimensional models, modal methods, machine-learning-augmented reduced-order models, and physics-informed approaches, identifying where each is effective and where further work is needed for various modelling challenges that arise in clinical problems.</p><p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2023.0565">Read the paper</a></p></div>

  <div><p class="section-label">In-silico trials</p><h2>FD-PASS: replicating and extending clinical trials</h2><p>Published in <em>Nature Communications</em> in 2021, FD-PASS provided a landmark demonstration that in-silico trials can reproduce and extend clinical evidence for medical devices. The study modelled flow-diverter treatment in 164 virtual patients with 82 distinct aneurysm anatomies, reproducing treatment-success rates from three clinical trials using a blood-flow surrogate for aneurysm occlusion. Controlled virtual experiments then examined branch vessels and hypertension, revealing factors associated with treatment failure and demonstrating the additional insight possible from virtual patient studies.</p><p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23998-w">Read the paper</a></p></div>
</section>
