<h1>Updates</h1>
<p class="lead">Research, presentations, and milestones.</p>

<ul class="news-list news-list-full">
  <li><time datetime="2026-10">October 2026</time><div><h2>New paper on risk-informed regulatory evaluation of medical devices</h2><p>Our paper, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.device.2026.101303">Risk-informed framework for in silico regulatory evaluation of medical devices</a>, was published in <em>Device</em>. The work presents a risk-informed framework for evaluating the credibility of in-silico evidence used in medical-device regulatory decision-making.</p></div></li>
  <li><time datetime="2026-09-16">September 2026</time><div><h2>Credibility assessment for computational flow-diverter models</h2><p>I presented an oral talk at the International Clinical Trials Methodology Conference in Birmingham on credibility assessment for computational models of flow-diverter devices in small intracranial aneurysms.</p><p>The presentation introduced in-silico trials and highlighted the FD-PASS and FD-PComA studies, which investigate flow-diverter treatment for intracranial aneurysms. A central focus of the talk was how UK CEiRSI Pilot 2 applied risk-informed credibility frameworks to assess the credibility of the models used in these in-silico trials. This work aims to establish confidence in simulation evidence for medical device development, clinical evaluation, and regulatory decision-making.</p></div></li>
  <li><time datetime="2026-06-05">June 2026</time><div><h2>New survey paper on medical image-to-mesh reconstruction</h2><p>Our new <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841526002227">survey paper</a> reviews deep-learning approaches for reconstructing three-dimensional anatomical meshes from medical images, covering template, statistical, generative, and implicit models alongside datasets, evaluation methods, current challenges, and future directions. Congratulations to lead author Fengming Lin and the co-authors on its publication.</p></div></li>
  <li><time datetime="2025-06-04">June 2025</time><div><h2>Two presentations at VITM 2025</h2><p>I gave an oral presentation on off-label in-silico flow-diverter performance assessment in posterior communicating artery aneurysms, and presented a poster on an automated modelling pipeline for fluid-structure interaction analyses in patients with severe aortic stenosis, at the Virtual Imaging Trials in Medicine Conference in Manchester.</p></div></li>
  <li><time datetime="2025-05-06">May 2025</time><div><h2>UK CEiRSI Pilot 2 panel session</h2><p>I contributed to the UK CEiRSI Pilot 2 panel session as Co-Lead Rapporteur. In this panel session, the Pilot 2 team discussed credibility assessment for computational models of haemodynamics used in assessing flow-diverter performance in off-label indications. <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jhPG1Ep1SGA&amp;t=352s">Watch the webinar</a>.</p></div></li>
  <li><time datetime="2025-03">March 2025</time><div><h2>New paper on in-silico neurosurgery</h2><p>Our paper, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wneu.2024.123589">In-silico neurosurgery: Toward safe, effective and equitable flow diverter treatment of intracranial aneurysms</a>, was published in <em>World Neurosurgery</em>. This article considers how in-silico trials can support safer and more equitable evaluation of flow-diverter treatment.</p></div></li>

My thesis explored efficient ensemble simulation methods for in-silico trials of endovascular medical devices: essentially, how to construct and accelerate computational models that can be used to understand medical device performance. I am grateful to my examiners, Professor Peter Jimack and Professor Alistair Revell, for making the three hours of questions go by as quickly and painlessly as possible!</p></div></li>
